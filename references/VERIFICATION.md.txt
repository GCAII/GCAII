# Charter for Decentralized Research Verification
**Reference Node:** `wiki/verification`  
**Associated File Standard:** `reference/VERIFICATION.md`  
**Parent Initiative:** Global Constrained AI Initiative (`GCAII`)

---

## Scope & Core Objectives

The **Charter for Decentralized Research Verification** establishes the foundational methodologies required to validate small language model (SLM) behavior, tensor-scale stability, and tool-calling fidelity across heterogeneous, resource-constrained hardware topologies. 

Operating under the GCAII standard that reproducible empirical verification supersedes centralized institutional authority, this charter provides independent research nodes and edge operators with transparent, hardware-aware validation standards designed to foster collaborative problem-solving.

---

## Collaborative Onboarding & Iterative Calibration

Recognizing that resource-constrained hardware topologies vary widely across independent nodes, the verification protocol rejects exclusionary gatekeeping in favor of collaborative onboarding:

- **Iterative Calibration Windows:** Newly integrated nodes are provided grace windows to calibrate local execution bounds, spectral rank metrics, and quantization thresholds alongside the broader federated network.
- **Remediation Over Rejection:** Encountered discrepancies or performance anomalies across independent nodes are treated as cooperative engineering challenges rather than compliance failures, encouraging joint root-cause analysis and shared patch deployment.

---

## Transparent Experimentation & Empirical Openness

To accelerate breakthroughs in sub-billion parameter Small Language Model efficiency, the verification standard actively encourages the documentation and sharing of empirical edge anomalies:

- **Sanctuary for Negative Results:** Nodes are encouraged to publish failed runs, quantization bottlenecks, and hardware limitation logs to the public record, treating negative experimental data as a critical asset for global optimization.
- **Adaptive Peer Review:** Community feedback loops prioritize peer-supported optimization over punitive evaluation, ensuring that independent research teams can test radical edge architectures safely.

---

## Infrastructure Protocols for Constrained Hardware Execution

To ensure reliable execution across diverse edge environments—ranging from legacy local compute nodes to memory-limited silicon—validation pipelines adhere to rigorous baseline standards:

- **Hardware-Aware Execution Bounds:** Execution protocols prioritize deterministic behavior over stochastic drift, supporting independent replication of layer-wise perplexity and spectral rank metrics across non-homogeneous silicon within stated tolerance bounds.
- **Resource-Constrained Optimization:** Verification routines are engineered to operate within strict memory and compute limits, allowing low-overhead auditing without violating local hardware constraints.

---

## Open Infrastructure Frameworks & Public Record Integrity

Verification data and benchmark evaluations are encouraged to remain transparently auditable to maintain the integrity of the GCAII public record:

- **Versioned Research Artifacts:** Empirical benchmark results, quantization stability metrics, and evaluation logs are archived via verified open-science repositories and assigned permanent digital object identifiers (DOIs).
- **Decentralized Traceability:** Any observed divergence in tool-calling stability or inference output across federated nodes must be accompanied by a transparent, inspectable trace rather than proprietary error reporting.

---

## Regulatory Interoperability in Algorithmic Architecture and Matrix Mechanics

To align mathematical performance metrics with cross-border technical standards, structural architecture parameters are evaluated through formal empirical frameworks:

- **Standardized Matrix Audits:** Layer-wise transformer audits, singular value decomposition (SVD) bounds, and activation gate stability are measured against unified open-source baselines.
- **Cross-Jurisdictional Alignment:** Computational verification protocols are designed to support technical audit needs across regional frameworks without requiring centralized cloud validation hooks.
